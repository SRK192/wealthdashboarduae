Is AED 25,000 a good salary in Dubai?

Yes. It is a strong salary on every frame: comfortably into the upper reaches of the national distribution and clearly above the middle of the professional cohort that recruiter salary guides describe. The precise answer depends on which population you compare against, and UAE salary statistics differ by a factor of five depending on that choice, so here are all four at once.

What jobs pay AED 25,000 a month in the UAE?

133 distinct job titles in our dataset of 837 benchmarked roles have a published salary band that contains AED 25,000 a month, across 28 sectors. A sample: AP/AR Manager, Accountant (qualified), Actuary, Airfield Operations Specialist, Analyst, Applications Support, Architect, Asset Manager, Assistant Manager, Assistant Relationship Manager – Support, Associate, Audit Manager.

If your own title is on that list, AED 25,000 is a normal market figure rather than a good or bad one. What moves you inside the band is experience, employer size and how tight hiring is in your sector when you negotiate. The salary guide by role gives the full published range for each title.

Can you live on AED 25,000 a month in Dubai?

A one-bedroom apartment in an affordable Dubai community runs about AED 56,000 to AED 61,000 a year, which is 20% of AED 300,000. That is comfortably inside the usual guidance of keeping rent under 30% of gross, leaving genuine room to save. A mid-tier one-bedroom in Business Bay at around AED 104,000 a year is a different conversation entirely.

Can a family of four live on AED 25,000 in Dubai?

A two-bedroom apartment in one of Dubai's more affordable communities such as Al Nahda or International City runs AED 72,000 to AED 82,000 a year. Two children in British-curriculum schools at about AED 96,000 a year bring housing and schooling to 59% of AED 300,000, inside the 60% test. A family of four is comfortable at this level provided the housing choice stays sensible: a two-bedroom in Business Bay or Dubai Marina at AED 148,000 to AED 157,000 changes the answer considerably.

School fees are the variable that decides this, not rent. Dubai annual fees average about AED 14,000 per child on the Indian curriculum, AED 42,000 on the American, AED 48,000 on the British and AED 65,000 on the IB. There is no public-school option for expatriate children, so this is a fixed cost rather than a choice.

Is AED 25,000 a good salary in Abu Dhabi or Sharjah?

Better in both, for different reasons. Abu Dhabi pays professional roles about 2% below Dubai but has a wider spread of housing, so the same AED 25,000 generally goes further. Sharjah is the bigger difference: a one-bedroom there runs about AED 37,000 to AED 43,000 a year against AED 56,000 to AED 61,000 in affordable Dubai, so roughly AED 18,000 a year stays in your pocket. The trade is the commute, which at peak times across Al Ittihad Road is substantial and is the reason many people who can afford Dubai still do not choose it.

What is AED 25,000 after tax in Dubai?

AED 25,000. There is no personal income tax on salaries anywhere in the UAE, so for an expatriate employee gross and net are the same number: AED 25,000 a month, AED 300,000 a year, with no statutory payroll deduction. Emirati nationals do have a pension deduction that expatriate staff do not, paid to the GPSSA or, in Abu Dhabi, to the Abu Dhabi Pension Fund. The 9% corporate tax introduced in June 2023 applies to business profits rather than employment income, and the 5% VAT is charged on spending.

How much gratuity does AED 25,000 accrue?

About AED 52,500 after five years of service. Under Article 51 of Federal Decree-Law 33 of 2021, end-of-service gratuity is 21 days of basic wage for each of the first five years and 30 days for each year after that, with a minimum of one year of service. This figure assumes basic pay is 60% of gross, a common UAE split but not a universal one, and gratuity is calculated on basic salary only, so allowances are excluded.

How does AED 25,000 compare with the UAE average and median?

AED 25,000 is about 180% above the median of AED 8,926 for workers in conventional households, which is the frame the official Labour Force Survey covers. Across the entire workforce the median is far lower at AED 3,662, because that count includes labour-camp and collective-household residents. Among white-collar professionals the median is AED 20,010. Published "average UAE salary" figures usually sit somewhere between these and rarely say which population they describe, which is why they disagree with each other so wildly.

What salary puts you in the top 10% or the top 1%?

Across all UAE workers, the top 10% starts at AED 21,674 a month and the top 1% at AED 87,333. Among white-collar professionals the bar is much higher: AED 55,520 for the top 10%.
